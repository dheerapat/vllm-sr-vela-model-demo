# Type 1 Diabetes

## Definition / Overview
- Type 1 diabetes (T1D) is an immune-mediated disease characterized by destruction of insulin-producing pancreatic β-cells, resulting in absolute insulin deficiency. Distinct from type 2 diabetes, it requires lifelong exogenous insulin replacement to prevent hyperglycemia and diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA). Management includes insulin therapy, glucose monitoring, education, and lifestyle modifications.

## Epidemiology
- T1D is one of the most frequent chronic diseases in children but can occur at any age.  
- Represents 5–10% of all diabetes cases.  
- Global incidence varies: highest in Finland and Northern Europe (~400 times higher than in China/Venezuela).  
- Incidence of DKA at diagnosis ranges from 15–70% in children.  
- Slight male predominance noted.  

## Etiology / Causes
- **Autoimmune destruction** of pancreatic β-cells in genetically susceptible individuals.  
- **Genetic factors**:  
  - Strong association with HLA class II alleles (DRB1, -DQA1, -DQB1), accounting for ~40% of familial aggregation.  
  - HLA-DR4-DQ8 and DR3-DQ2 present in ~90% of children with T1D.  
- **Environmental triggers**:  
  - Viral infections (Coxsackie virus, enteroviruses, cytomegalovirus, rubella, influenza B, mumps, SARS-CoV-2).  
  - Dietary factors (cow’s milk, cereal exposure), childhood vaccinations, pregnancy/perinatal conditions.  

## Pathophysiology
- **Stage 1 (Preclinical)**: Asymptomatic; normal glucose tolerance, ≥2 pancreatic autoantibodies.  
- **Stage 2 (Dysglycemia)**: Impaired fasting glucose (100–125 mg/dL), impaired glucose tolerance (140–199 mg/dL 2-hour post-OGTT), or HbA1c 5.7–6.4%.  
- **Stage 3 (Clinical onset)**: Symptomatic hyperglycemia with HbA1c ≥6.5%, fasting glucose ≥126 mg/dL, or random glucose ≥200 mg/dL.  

## Clinical Presentation
- Classic symptoms: polyuria, polydipsia, unintentional weight loss, fatigue, weakness.  
- DKA: fruity-smelling breath, lethargy, coma (more common in children).  
- Adults may present with gradual progression or initial misdiagnosis as type 2 diabetes.  

## Evaluation / Diagnosis
- **Diagnostic criteria for diabetes**:  
  - Fasting plasma glucose ≥126 mg/dL (confirmed).  
  - Random plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL with symptoms.  
  - 2-hour post-75g OGTT ≥200 mg/dL.  
  - HbA1c ≥6.5%.  
- **T1D differentiation**:  
  - Autoantibody testing (GAD65, IA-2, ZnT8, ICA, IAA).  
  - C-peptide levels to assess β-cell function.  
- Regular HbA1c monitoring (every 3 months).  

## Differential Diagnosis
- Type 2 diabetes  
- Monogenic diabetes  
- Cystic fibrosis-related diabetes  
- Chronic pancreatitis-related diabetes  
- Posttransplantation diabetes mellitus  
- Steroid-induced diabetes  
- Psychogenic polydipsia  

## Management / Treatment
- **Insulin replacement**:  
  - **Multiple daily injections (MDI)**: Basal (long-acting: glargine, detemir, degludec) + prandial (rapid-acting: lispro, aspart, glulisine).  
  - **Continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII)**: Insulin pump with basal + bolus dosing; advanced systems include CGM-integrated automated delivery.  
- **Glucose monitoring**:  
  - Capillary blood glucose meters (≥4 times daily).  
  - Continuous glucose monitors (CGMs) for real-time data and hypoglycemia alerts.  
- **Education**:  
  - Carbohydrate counting, insulin-to-carb ratios.  
  - Hypoglycemia recognition/treatment (glucagon for severe cases).  
  - Sick day rules, ketone testing.  
- **Lifestyle**: Physical activity guidance, consistent carbohydrate intake.  

## Complications
- **Acute**: Hypoglycemia, DKA.  
- **Chronic**: Nephropathy, neuropathy (peripheral/autonomic), retinopathy, cardiovascular disease (coronary artery disease, heart failure), peripheral arterial disease, stroke, foot infections/ulcers.  

## Prognosis
- Improved morbidity/mortality with modern insulin therapy and metabolic control.  
- 2–5 times higher mortality risk compared to the general population.  
- Timely complication screening and glycemic control reduce severe outcomes.  

## Prevention / Patient Education
- Importance of blood glucose control, complication screening, and lifestyle modifications.  
- Education on hypoglycemia symptoms and treatment.  
- Encourage support groups and adherence to follow-up.  

## Interprofessional Considerations
- **Team approach**: Endocrinologists, diabetes educators, nurses, dietitians, pharmacists, mental health professionals, podiatrists, ophthalmologists, social workers.  
- **Roles**:  
  - Nurses: Care coordination, patient education, monitoring.  
  - Pharmacists: Insulin dosing, medication reconciliation.  
  - Dietitians: Carbohydrate management, nutrition planning.  
  - Mental health professionals: Address diabetes distress, eating disorders.  
- Emphasis on individualized treatment plans and collaborative communication.
