# Arthritis  

## Definition / Overview  
- Arthritis is defined as acute or chronic joint inflammation, often accompanied by pain and structural damage.  
- Not synonymous with arthralgia (joint pain without inflammation).  
- Over 100 types exist; most common include osteoarthritis (non-inflammatory) and inflammatory types such as rheumatoid arthritis (RA), psoriatic arthritis, gout, pseudogout, and septic arthritis.  

## Epidemiology  
- Over one-third of the American population shows arthritis on imaging.  
- **Osteoarthritis**:  
  - 19–30% of adults >45 years have knee osteoarthritis.  
  - 27% have hand or hip osteoarthritis.  
  - Lifetime risk: 40% in men, 47% in women; increases to 60% with BMI >30.  
- **Gout**:  
  - Affects >8 million in the US (prevalence 3.9%), rising to >9% in those >60 years.  
  - Incidence >45/100,000 and increasing.  
- **Rheumatoid arthritis**:  
  - Prevalence ~1% in Caucasians; female lifetime risk 3.6% vs. 1.7% in males.  
- **Septic arthritis**:  
  - Prevalence 0.01% in general population, 0.7% in RA patients.  

## Etiology / Causes  
- **Osteoarthritis**:  
  - Advancing age, female sex, joint trauma, obesity, genetic mutations in collagen-encoding genes.  
- **Rheumatoid arthritis**:  
  - Autoimmune disorder linked to HLA-DRB1 genes and environmental factors (e.g., smoking).  
- **Gout**:  
  - Prolonged hyperuricemia (genetic mutations <10%), male sex, aging, CKD, alcoholism, diuretics.  
- **Septic arthritis**:  
  - Bacterial seeding (often *Staphylococcus aureus* or streptococci) via hematogenous spread.  
- **Autoimmune associations**:  
  - SLE, Sjogren syndrome, scleroderma, IBD, celiac disease, etc.  

## Pathophysiology  
- **Osteoarthritis**:  
  - Degenerative cartilage loss, subchondral cysts, osteophytes, and interleukin-mediated matrix degradation.  
- **Rheumatoid arthritis**:  
  - Autoimmune synovial inflammation, cytokine release (TNF-α, IL-1, 6, 8), cartilage/bone erosion.  
- **Gout**:  
  - Monosodium urate crystal deposition → IL-1-mediated inflammation.  
- **Pseudogout**:  
  - Calcium pyrophosphate dihydrate crystals → joint damage (osteophytes, subchondral cysts).  
- **Septic arthritis**:  
  - Bacterial toxins/cytokines cause synovial hyperplasia and cartilage degradation.  

## Clinical Presentation  
- **Symptoms**: Pain, stiffness, decreased range of motion, joint deformities, fatigue, sleep disturbances.  
- **Onset**:  
  - Acute: septic, crystalline, reactive arthritis.  
  - Insidious: OA, RA, psoriatic arthritis.  
- **Joint involvement**:  
  - Monoarticular (e.g., gout, septic), oligoarticular (HLA-B27 spondyloarthropathies), polyarticular (RA, SLE).  
- **Physical findings**:  
  - Inflammatory: swelling, effusion, erythema, warmth.  
  - Non-inflammatory: tenderness, mechanical symptoms (OA).  

## Evaluation / Diagnosis  
- **History/physical**: Assess articular vs. non-articular pain, morning stiffness, joint distribution.  
- **Labs**:  
  - Inflammatory markers (ESR, CRP), uric acid, RF, anti-CCP, ANA.  
  - Synovial fluid analysis: cell count, crystals (gout: needle-shaped, negatively birefringent; pseudogout: rhomboid, positively birefringent), cultures.  
- **Imaging**:  
  - X-ray: osteophytes (OA), erosions (RA), chondrocalcinosis (pseudogout).  
  - MRI/CT/ultrasound for detailed soft tissue/bony assessment.  

## Differential Diagnosis  
- Non-articular pain (fibromyalgia, myofascial pain, neuropathy, tendinitis).  
- Over 100 arthritis subtypes requiring precise diagnosis before treatment.  

## Management / Treatment  
- **Osteoarthritis**:  
  - Non-pharmacologic: exercise, PT, weight loss, bracing.  
  - Pharmacologic: NSAIDs, capsaicin, duloxetine, intra-articular corticosteroids.  
  - Surgical: joint replacement for refractory cases.  
- **Rheumatoid arthritis**:  
  - DMARDs (e.g., methotrexate), biologics, NSAIDs, glucocorticoids.  
- **Gout**:  
  - Acute: NSAIDs, colchicine, corticosteroids.  
  - Chronic: urate-lowering therapies (e.g., allopurinol).  
- **Septic arthritis**:  
  - Joint drainage and antibiotics.  

## Complications  
- Not specified in source.  

## Prognosis  
- **Osteoarthritis**: Progressive; outcomes improve post-replacement but prostheses require revision (10–15 years).  
- **Rheumatoid arthritis**: Improved with early DMARDs/biologics; higher mortality due to extra-articular manifestations.  

## Prevention / Patient Education  
- Weight loss, exercise, muscle strengthening, diet modification.  
- Medication adherence, smoking cessation, alcohol avoidance.  
- Collaboration with dietitians, PTs, and interdisciplinary teams.  

## Interprofessional Considerations  
- Team includes rheumatologists, orthopedic surgeons, PTs, pharmacists, nurses, and pain specialists.  
- Focus on polypharmacy monitoring, rehabilitation, and early intervention to reduce narcotic dependence.  
- Guidelines recommend interprofessional collaboration (e.g., American College of Rheumatology).
