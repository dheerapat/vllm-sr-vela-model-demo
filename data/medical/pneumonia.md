# Bacterial Pneumonia

## Definition / Overview
- Inflammation of lung parenchyma and alveolar spaces caused by various bacteria, triggering an intricate interplay with the host immune response.

## Epidemiology
- Global annual incidence: 450 million CAP cases, 95% in developing countries, contributing to ~4 million deaths annually.
- Europe: 206–470 cases/100,000 patients/year; China: 298–2210 cases/100 patient-years.
- US: 649–847 CAP hospitalizations/100,000 patient-years, disproportionately affecting low-income and minority groups.
- Highest incidence in ages <4 years and ≥65 years.
- US estimates: 2.2–8 million annual hospitalizations; CAP-related mortality: 100,000 deaths/year.
- Global CAP mortality: 2.6–18.5% at hospitalization, up to 31–44.5% within 1 year.

## Etiology / Causes
- **Community-Acquired Pneumonia (CAP):** 
  - *Streptococcus pneumoniae*, *Haemophilus influenzae*, *Staphylococcus aureus*, Group A *Streptococci*, *Moraxella catarrhalis*, anaerobes, aerobic Gram-negative bacteria (e.g., *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*).
  - Atypical: *Legionella* spp., *Mycoplasma pneumoniae*, *Chlamydia pneumoniae*, *Chlamydia psittaci*.
  - Opportunistic: *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, *Nocardia* spp.
- **Hospital-Acquired Pneumonia (HAP)/Ventilator-Associated Pneumonia (VAP):** 
  - *K. pneumoniae*, *E. coli*, *P. aeruginosa*, *S. aureus* (including MRSA), *Enterobacter* spp., *Acinetobacter baumannii* complex.

## Pathophysiology
- Lower respiratory tract exposed to environmental pathogens. Bacterial invasion triggers innate immune response: 
  - Toll-like receptor recognition → neutrophilic infiltration.
  - Alveolar macrophages attempt bacterial clearance.
- Bacterial virulence factors: 
  - Polysaccharide capsules (*S. pneumoniae*), adhesion proteins (*M. pneumoniae*), pili (*Legionella pneumophila*).
- Inflammatory cytokines (IL-1, TNF, IL-6, IL-8, G-CSF) drive symptoms: fever, leukocytosis, purulent secretions.
- Alveolar-capillary membrane leakage → hypoxia, fibrosis, dyspnea.
- Pleuritic pain via phrenic nerve irritation; hemoptysis from parenchymal necrosis.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Symptoms:** Fever, cough (productive/nonproductive), dyspnea, chest pain, fatigue, headache, myalgia, arthralgia.
- **Physical findings:**
  - Fever/hypothermia, tachypnea, tachycardia/bradycardia, cyanosis, decreased chest expansion, dull percussion, crackles/rales, bronchial breath sounds, egophony, hypoxia.
  - **Infants:** Tachypnea, nasal flaring, intercostal retraction, abdominal distension.
  - **Children <5 years:** Tachypnea, chest indrawing, abdominal pain.
  - **Elderly:** Early confusion, reduced fever/rigor/sputum production.
  - **Etiology-specific signs:** Relative bradycardia (*Legionella*), dental issues (anaerobes), gag reflex impairment (aspiration pneumonia).

## Evaluation / Diagnosis
- **Clinical assessment:** Prognostic scores (CURB-65, CORB, SMART-COP, PSI).
- **Laboratory:** CBC, CRP, blood cultures (in severe cases), sputum Gram stain/culture, urine antigen/PCR for specific pathogens.
- **Radiological:** Chest X-ray (consolidations, parapneumonic effusions) as gold standard; CT for complex cases.
- **Bronchoalveolar lavage:** In intubated patients.

## Differential Diagnosis
- **Children:** Asthma, bronchiolitis, croup, epiglottitis, respiratory distress syndrome.
- **Adults:** Acute/chronic bronchitis, acute pulmonary edema, ARDS, foreign body aspiration, asthma, atelectasis, bronchiectasis, COPD exacerbation, fungal/PCP pneumonia, interstitial lung disease, organizing pneumonia, respiratory failure, viral pneumonia.

## Management / Treatment
- **Risk stratification:** CURB-65, clinical judgment.
- **Empirical antibiotics:** 
  - Outpatients (no comorbidities): Penicillin, macrolide, tetracycline.
  - Outpatients (comorbidities): Respiratory fluoroquinolone or amoxicillin/clavulanate + macrolide.
  - Inpatients: Beta-lactam + macrolide or respiratory fluoroquinolone; adjust per susceptibility.
  - *P. aeruginosa*: Piperacillin-tazobactam, ceftazidime, cefepime, ciprofloxacin, meropenem.
- **Adjunctive therapies:** Antipyretics, corticosteroids (controversial; consider in severe CAP), chest physiotherapy (limited evidence).
- **Nonpharmacological:** Smoking cessation counseling, vaccination (influenza, pneumococcus, COVID-19), dysphagia management, early mobilization, hydration, nutrition, positioning (lateral decubitus in consolidation).

## Complications
- Lung fibrosis, necrotizing pneumonia, empyema, pulmonary abscess, cavitation, meningitis, sepsis, multiorgan failure, death.

## Prognosis
- Influenced by age, comorbidities (malignancy, COPD), immunosuppression, alcohol misuse, hospital setting, ventilatory support.
- *S. pneumoniae* mortality: ~30% untreated; higher with *Pseudomonas*, MRSA, polymicrobial infections.
- Predictors of poor prognosis: PSI score, persistent tachypnea/hypoxia/confusion/acidosis, APACHE II >21 or SOFA >6 in VAP.

## Prevention / Patient Education
- Smoking cessation, alcohol moderation, dental hygiene, hand hygiene.
- Vaccination: Pneumococcal (older adults/immunocompromised), annual influenza vaccine.
- Early medical attention for high-risk groups (dyspnea, rigors, fever).

## Interprofessional Considerations
- Multidisciplinary approach: Physicians, nurses (VAP prevention, vaccination), pharmacists (antimicrobial stewardship), physiotherapists (mobility), dieticians (nutrition), speech therapists (dysphagia), psychologists (substance use).
- Early mobilization, oral care in aged care facilities, comorbidity management, and follow-up critical.
