# Obstructive Sleep Apnea Syndrome  

## Definition / Overview  
- Obstructive sleep apnea syndrome (OSA) is defined by frequent episodes of apnea and hypopnea associated with symptoms such as excessive daytime sleepiness and cardiovascular morbidity/mortality.  
- Characterized by dynamic collapse of upper airway tissues during sleep, leading to recurrent respiratory events and both short-term symptomatic and long-term physiologic consequences.  

## Epidemiology  
- Prevalence ranges from 21-59% in the general population and patients with cardiac disease, respectively.  
- Most common in adult males, but post-menopausal women have rates approaching those of men.  
- In North America: 15-30% in males, 10-15% in females.  
- Higher prevalence in African Americans, independent of body weight.  
- Increasing prevalence attributed to rising obesity rates and improved diagnosis.  

## Etiology / Causes  
- **Craniofacial factors**: Retrognathia, maxillomandibular deficiency, reduced upper airway patency due to skeletal structure.  
- **Soft tissue factors**: Increased submucosal/lingual adipose tissue, cervical lymphovascular tone, reduced neuromuscular upper airway tone.  
- **Risk factors**: Obesity, male gender, advanced age, smoking, family history of OSA.  

## Pathophysiology  
- Decreased tonic activity of upper airway muscles (laryngeal, supraglottic, oropharyngeal, nasopharyngeal, lingual) during sleep, causing dynamic airway collapse.  
- Obstruction sites: nasal cavity, nasopharynx, oropharynx, base of tongue, laryngeal complex (may involve multiple levels).  
- **Respiratory events**:  
  - Apnea: Cessation of airflow ≥10 seconds.  
  - Hypopnea: ≥30% airflow reduction with ≥4% oxygen desaturation *or* ≥50% reduction with ≥3% desaturation/arousal.  
  - RERA: Breath sequence with increasing effort/flattened waveform causing arousal.  

## Clinical Presentation  
- **Common symptoms**: Daytime sleepiness (difficulty staying alert), loud snoring, witnessed apneas/choking, morning headaches.  
- **Less common**: Insomnia, chest pain/palpitations, neuropsychiatric changes (mood disorders, cognitive dysfunction), nocturia.  

## Evaluation / Diagnosis  
- **History and physical**: Assess symptoms, sleep hygiene, craniofacial anatomy, nasal/tongue abnormalities, tonsillar hypertrophy.  
  - Tools: Updated Friedmann Staging System, flexible fiberoptic laryngoscopy (awake vs. drug-induced sleep endoscopy [DISE]).  
- **Diagnostic testing**:  
  - **Epworth Sleepiness Scale**: Score >10 suggestive of OSA.  
  - **Polysomnography**: Gold standard; calculates:  
    - Apnea-Hypopnea Index (AHI) ≥5 with symptoms *or* AHI ≥15 without symptoms confirms diagnosis.  
    - Severity: Mild (5–14), moderate (15–30), severe (>30).  
  - Home sleep apnea tests: Alternative for select patients.  

## Differential Diagnosis  
- Central sleep apnea, primary snoring, laryngospasm, narcolepsy, periodic limb movement disorder, restless leg syndrome, circadian rhythm disorders, sleep hygiene-related issues.  

## Management / Treatment  
### **Education and Behavior**  
- Counsel on risks, complications, motor vehicle collision (MVC) risk, and anesthesia/sedation precautions.  
- Behavioral modifications: Weight loss, exercise, diet, sleep position adjustment, avoid alcohol/sedatives.  

### **Positive Airway Pressure Therapy**  
- **CPAP/BiPAP/APAP**: First-line therapy; improves event frequency, daytime sleepiness, blood pressure, quality of life.  
- Adherence challenges: 20-40% non-adherence.  

### **Alternative/Adjunctive Therapies**  
- **Oral appliances**: Mandibular advancement devices for mild-moderate OSA (dental side effects possible).  
- **Surgery**: Tonsillectomy, uvulopalatopharyngoplasty, maxillomandibular advancement, hypoglossal nerve stimulation (BMI <32, moderate-severe OSA), tracheostomy (last-resort).  

## Complications  
- Increased MVC risk (2-3× general population).  
- Neuropsychiatric: Cognitive deficits, mood disorders, sexual dysfunction.  
- Cardiopulmonary: Hypertension, coronary artery disease, arrhythmias, heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, stroke.  
- Metabolic: Insulin resistance, type 2 diabetes complications.  
- Hepatic: Non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (2-3× increased incidence).  

## Prognosis  
- Short-term prognosis favorable with treatment.  
- Untreated OSA linked to increased mortality from cardiopulmonary complications, MVCs, and neurocognitive decline.  
- Treatment improves symptoms but not long-term cardiovascular/mortality outcomes.  

## Prevention / Patient Education  
- Screen for symptoms and risk factors.  
- Educate on pathophysiology, treatment options, lifestyle modifications, and long-term monitoring.  

## Interprofessional Considerations  
- Multidisciplinary care involving clinicians, nurses, sleep specialists, and pharmacists for comprehensive management.  
- Coordination ensures accurate diagnosis, adherence to therapy, and monitoring for complications.  
- Patient counseling and data sharing among team members are critical for optimal outcomes.
