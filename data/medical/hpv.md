# Human Papillomavirus (HPV)

## Definition / Overview
- Human papillomavirus (HPV) is a non-enveloped, double-stranded, circular DNA virus causing epithelial lesions (e.g., cutaneous and anogenital warts) and cancers. Subtypes vary in oncogenic potential, with high-risk types (e.g., 16, 18) linked to malignancies.

## Epidemiology
- Over 180 HPV subtypes exist, with tropism for specific body sites:
  - **Cutaneous warts**: Subtypes 1, 2, 4, 27, 57 (hands, feet).
  - **Anogenital warts**: Subtypes 6, 11 (low-risk); subtypes 16, 18, 31, 33, 45, 52, 58 (high-risk, linked to cervical/anogenital/oropharyngeal cancers).
- **CDC data**: Genital HPV prevalence ~45.2% (men) and ~39.9% (women) aged 18–59.
- Transmission: Skin-to-skin contact, sexual transmission, fomites.

## Etiology / Causes
- **Virus characteristics**: Papillomaviridae family; enters via skin/mucosal disruption, infects basal stem cells.
- **Genome**: Early (E1–E7) and late (L1, L2) genes. E6/E7 oncoproteins drive carcinogenesis.
- **Risk factors**: 
  - Sexual activity, early initiation, multiple partners.
  - Smoking, oral contraceptives (>5 years), betel nut use, radiation/UV exposure.

## Pathophysiology
- **E6/E7 proteins**: Inactivate tumor suppressors p53 and pRb, disrupting cell cycle regulation.
- **Viral lifecycle**: Remains as episome initially; integrates into host DNA at fragile sites.
- **Histopathology**: Hyperkeratosis, papillomatosis, parakeratosis; thrombosed capillaries, elongated rete ridges.

## Clinical Presentation
- **Lesions**: 
  - Cutaneous (verruca vulgaris/plantaris), anogenital (condyloma acuminatum), oropharyngeal.
  - Latent infections may require DNA testing for detection.
- **High-risk subtypes**: Linked to cervical, anal, oropharyngeal cancers and precancerous lesions (e.g., cervical dysplasia).

## Evaluation / Diagnosis
- **History**: 
  - Cutaneous warts: Hygiene, infectious contacts, pain/bleeding.
  - Anogenital warts: Sexual history, immunocompromise, prior treatment/vaccination.
  - Cervical dysplasia: Menstrual history, Pap smear results, symptoms (bleeding, pain).
- **Physical exam**: 
  - Cutaneous: Hands/feet inspection (palms, soles, interdigital spaces).
  - Anogenital: Speculum/vaginal/anal exam; oropharyngeal exam if indicated.
- **Tests**: 
  - Pap smear (cervical/anal), HPV DNA testing, colposcopy (acetowhite lesions biopsied).
  - Histopathology for excised lesions.

## Differential Diagnosis
- Corns & calluses, acrochordon, condyloma latum, keratoacanthoma, psoriasis, seborrhea, chancroid, herpes simplex, molluscum contagiosum.

## Management / Treatment
- **Cutaneous warts**: 
  - Cryotherapy, surgical removal, immunomodulators (e.g., imiquimod), laser ablation.
- **Anogenital warts**: Similar to cutaneous; consider patient immunocompetence.
- **Cervical dysplasia**:
  - Low-grade/immunocompetent: Monitoring (Pap/HPV testing, colposcopy).
  - High-grade/persistent: Cryotherapy, LEEP, cold knife cone excision.
- **Cancer**: Resection, chemotherapy, radiation.
- **Vaccination**: 9-valent vaccine (subtypes 6,11,16,18,31,33,45,52,58); recommended ages 9–12, females ≤26, males ≤21.

## Complications
- Poor cosmesis, depression, urethral obstruction, ulceration/infection of condylomata, progression to malignancy, transmission to others, co-infection with other STIs (5–20% risk).

## Prognosis
- Recurrences common; ~60% genital warts resolve spontaneously. 
- Risk of cervical, anal, head/neck cancers, especially in immunocompromised individuals.

## Prevention / Patient Education
- HPV vaccination (preferrably pre-sexual activity).
- Safe sex practices (condoms, limited partners), smoking cessation.
- Regular Pap smears and HPV testing for cervical cancer screening.
- Avoid betel nut, UV/radiation exposure.

## Interprofessional Considerations
- **Team roles**: 
  - **Infectious disease**: Immunocompromised patients.
  - **ENT**: Oropharyngeal papillomas.
  - **Urology**: Urethral/penile lesions.
  - **Colorectal surgery**: Anal disease.
  - **Dermatology**: Warts/epidermodysplasia verruciformis.
- **Education**: Nurses/pharmacists counsel on vaccination, safe sex, treatment options, and STI screening. Primary care providers emphasize Pap screening and risk reduction.
