# Inflammatory Bowel Disease

## Definition / Overview
- Chronic inflammatory disease of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract, divided into Crohn disease (CD) and ulcerative colitis (UC).  
- Characterized by repetitive episodes of inflammation due to an abnormal immune response to gut microflora.  
- UC involves diffuse mucosal inflammation of the colon, often starting in the rectum and extending proximally.  
- CD causes transmural ulceration of any GI segment, with skip lesions and possible fistulas/strictures.  

## Epidemiology
- **Incidence (North America):**  
  - UC: 2.2–19.2/100,000 person-years.  
  - CD: 3.1–20.2/100,000 person-years.  
- **Prevalence (US adults):**  
  - UC: 238/100,000.  
  - CD: 201/100,000.  
- More prevalent in North America/Europe vs. Asia/Africa.  
- Bimodal age distribution: 15–30 years (75%) and 10–15% after age 60.  
- CD slightly more common in females; UC equal gender distribution.  

## Etiology / Causes
- Genetic susceptibility combined with exaggerated immune response to intestinal flora.  
- **Crohn disease:** Strong link to tobacco use.  
- **Ulcerative colitis:** Smoking may be protective.  
- Diet role debated; *CARD15* gene associated with CD (not UC).  

## Pathophysiology
- Defective intestinal epithelial barrier (primary failure or inflammation-induced).  
- Impaired mucus production and antimicrobial peptide secretion (e.g., alpha-defensins).  
- Dysregulated inflammation: Elevated TNF-α, IL-1β, IL-23-TH17 pathway cytokines.  
- **UC:** Mucosal inflammation (edema, ulcers, bleeding), continuous from rectum, "lead-pipe" colon in chronic cases.  
- **CD:** Transmural inflammation, skip lesions, cobblestone mucosa, granulomas possible.  
- Extraintestinal manifestations (e.g., arthritis, primary sclerosing cholangitis).  

## Clinical Presentation
- **UC:**  
  - Bloody diarrhea (with/without mucus), tenesmus, abdominal pain (left-sided).  
  - Constipation possible in rectal disease.  
- **CD:**  
  - Right lower quadrant pain, non-bloody diarrhea, weight loss, fever.  
  - Fistulas (e.g., rectovaginal), abscesses, growth retardation in children.  
- **Physical exam:** Tachycardia, fever, dehydration, abdominal tenderness. Toxic megacolon: distension, guarding, rebound tenderness.  

## Evaluation / Diagnosis
- **Laboratory tests:**  
  - CBC (anemia, leukocytosis), ESR, hs-CRP, albumin.  
  - Fecal calprotectin/lactoferrin for inflammation.  
  - Serology: p-ANCA (UC), ASCA (CD).  
  - Stool studies: Rule out infection (*Giardia*, *C. difficile*, ova/parasites).  
- **Imaging:**  
  - X-ray: Free air, obstruction, toxic megacolon.  
  - Barium: "Lead pipe" colon (UC), skip lesions (CD).  
  - CT/MRI: Assess perforation, fistulas, strictures.  
- **Endoscopy:** Colonoscopy/EGD with biopsies (crypt abscesses in UC, granulomas in CD).  

## Differential Diagnosis
- Infectious colitis (*E. coli*, *C. difficile*), microscopic/lymphocytic/collagenous colitis.  
- Appendicitis, celiac disease, irritable bowel syndrome, functional abdominal pain.  

## Management / Treatment
- **Stepwise approach:**  
  1. Aminosalicylates (mesalamine) for mild UC/CD.  
  2. Corticosteroids (oral/systemic) for moderate disease.  
  3. Immunomodulators (anti-TNF agents, azathioprine, methotrexate) for steroid-refractory/severe disease.  
- **UC-specific:** Topical mesalamine for proctitis; colectomy for uncontrolled disease.  
- **CD-specific:** Budesonide for ileocecal disease; surgery for fistulas/strictures.  
- **General:** Bone density monitoring (steroid use: calcium/bisphosphonates).  

## Complications
- **Intestinal:** Hemorrhage, strictures, perforation, toxic megacolon, cholangiocarcinoma, colon cancer.  
- **Extraintestinal:** Osteoporosis, DVT, gallstones, primary sclerosing cholangitis, arthritis, pyoderma gangrenosum.  

## Prognosis
- Mortality 1.5–5× higher than general population.  
- Colon cancer risk: Cumulative 30% in UC pancolitis >30 years.  
- Psychological morbidity (depression, anxiety) common.  

## Prevention / Patient Education
- No diet/supplement prevents symptoms.  
- Lifelong follow-up for disease monitoring and dysplasia surveillance.  
- Education improves compliance and quality of life.  

## Interprofessional Considerations
- Interprofessional team essential for comprehensive care (gastroenterologists, surgeons, nurses, pharmacists).  
- Dedicated support lines and accessible provider networks for emergencies.  
- Early collaboration to prevent bowel damage and disability.  
- Address psychological health; monitor for depression/anxiety.
