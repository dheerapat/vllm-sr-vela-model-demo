# Chronic Kidney Disease

## Definition / Overview
- Chronic kidney disease (CKD) is defined by kidney damage or an estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) <60 mL/min/1.73 m² persisting for ≥3 months.  
- Classified by the 2012 KDIGO criteria into 6 stages (G1–G5) based on eGFR and 3 stages of albuminuria (A1–A3) based on urinary albumin-creatinine ratio (ACR):  
  - **G1**: eGFR ≥90 mL/min/1.73 m² with kidney damage  
  - **G2**: eGFR 60–89 mL/min/1.73 m²  
  - **G3a**: eGFR 45–59 mL/min/1.73 m²  
  - **G3b**: eGFR 30–44 mL/min/1.73 m²  
  - **G4**: eGFR 15–29 mL/min/1.73 m²  
  - **G5**: eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m² or dialysis-dependent  
  - **A1**: ACR <30 mg/g (<3.4 mg/mmol)  
  - **A2**: ACR 30–299 mg/g (3.4–34 mg/mmol)  
  - **A3**: ACR >300 mg/g (>34 mg/mmol)  

## Epidemiology
- Global prevalence: ~10–14%, with albuminuria (~7%) and eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² (~4%) contributing.  
- In the U.S. (2016): ~26 million affected.  
- Contributed to 2,968,600 disability-adjusted life-years and 2,546,700 life-years lost globally in 2012.  

## Etiology / Causes
- **Primary diseases leading to ESRD**:  
  - Type 2 diabetes (30–50%)  
  - Type 1 diabetes (3.9%)  
  - Hypertension (27.2%)  
  - Primary glomerulonephritis (8.2%)  
  - Chronic tubulointerstitial nephritis (3.6%)  
  - Hereditary/cystic diseases (3.1%)  
  - Secondary glomerulonephritis/vasculitis (2.1%)  
  - Plasma cell dyscrasias/neoplasm (2.1%)  
  - Sickle cell nephropathy (<1% in the U.S.)  
- **Categories of disease processes**:  
  - **Prerenal**: Chronic heart failure, cirrhosis, leading to ischemic nephropathy.  
  - **Intrinsic renal**:  
    - Vascular (e.g., nephrosclerosis, renal artery stenosis).  
    - Glomerular (nephritic/nephrotic syndromes; e.g., post-infectious GN, IgA nephropathy, diabetic nephropathy).  
    - Tubulointerstitial (e.g., polycystic kidney disease, MesoAmerican nephropathy).  
  - **Postrenal**: Prostatic disease, nephrolithiasis, congenital anomalies, retroperitoneal fibrosis.  

## Pathophysiology
- Progressive fibrosis and destruction of renal architecture: glomerulosclerosis, tubulointerstitial fibrosis, vascular sclerosis.  
- Mechanisms:  
  - Inflammatory cell infiltration.  
  - Activation/apoptosis of renal cells.  
  - Extracellular matrix deposition.  
- Accelerating factors:  
  - Systemic/intraglomerular hypertension.  
  - Glomerular hypertrophy.  
  - Calcium phosphate precipitation.  
  - Altered prostanoid metabolism.  

## Clinical Presentation
- **Asymptomatic in early stages (1–3)**. Symptoms in stages 4–5:  
  - Nausea, vomiting, anorexia, fatigue, sleep disturbances, oliguria, muscle cramps, edema, pruritus, cognitive changes, pericarditis, pulmonary edema, hypertension.  
- **Physical findings**: Skin pigmentation, excoriations, pericardial rub, uremic frost, hyperreflexia, hypertensive retinopathy.  

## Evaluation / Diagnosis
- **Establishing chronicity**:  
  - History of hypertension, proteinuria, or structural abnormalities.  
  - Persistent eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² for ≥3 months.  
  - Imaging (e.g., small kidneys with cortical thinning on ultrasound).  
- **Assessment**:  
  - eGFR (using CKD-EPI equation).  
  - Urine ACR (early morning sample).  
  - Imaging: Ultrasound for kidney size/cysts, Doppler for renal artery stenosis.  
  - Biopsy for definitive etiology.  

## Differential Diagnosis  
- Acute kidney injury  
- Alport syndrome  
- Antiglomerular basement membrane disease  
- Diabetic nephropathy  
- Multiple myeloma  
- Nephrolithiasis  
- Rapidly progressive glomerulonephritis  
- Renal artery stenosis  

## Management / Treatment
- **General**:  
  - Adjust drug dosages based on eGFR.  
  - Avoid nephrotoxins (e.g., NSAIDs, aminoglycosides).  
- **Slowing progression**:  
  - Control hypertension (systolic <120 mmHg per KDIGO 2021; <130/80 mmHg per ACC/AHA 2017).  
  - Reduce proteinuria (<1 g/d if possible).  
  - Address metabolic acidosis (bicarbonate supplementation to maintain serum level ~23 mEq/L).  
  - Smoking cessation, dietary protein restriction, glycemic control in diabetes.  
- **Renal replacement therapy (RRT)**:  
  - Initiate when eGFR <15 mL/min/1.73 m² or emergent indications (e.g., pericarditis, refractory hyperkalemia).  
  - Options: Hemodialysis (AV fistula preferred), peritoneal dialysis, transplantation.  
- **Indications for RRT**:  
  - Uremic pericarditis/encephalopathy, bleeding diathesis, refractory fluid overload/metabolic disorders.  

## Complications
- **Systemic**:  
  - Fluid overload, hypertension, hyperkalemia, metabolic acidosis, hyperphosphatemia, anemia, cardiovascular disease.  
- **Mineral bone disorder**: Secondary hyperparathyroidism, FGF23 elevation.  
- **Transplant-related**: Infections, malignancies, cardiovascular/metabolic complications.  

## Prognosis
- Community-based CKD progresses slowly (eGFR decline ~0.75–1 mL/min/year); 24–45% of stages 3–4 die from cardiovascular events before ESRD.  
- Faster progression in referred CKD (e.g., diabetic nephropathy: ~10 mL/min/year decline).  

## Prevention / Patient Education
- Screen high-risk groups (diabetes, hypertension, age >65) via urinalysis, ACR, eGFR.  
- Patient education: BP monitoring, low-sodium diet, avoid nephrotoxins, dietary counseling, pregnancy risks (teratogenicity of ACE inhibitors).  

## Interprofessional Considerations
- Nephrologists, primary care providers, dietitians, pharmacists, and nurses collaborate to manage risk factors, adjust medications, provide dialysis access, and coordinate transplantation.  
- Multidisciplinary care reduces hospitalizations, improves vascular access placement, and enhances outcomes.
