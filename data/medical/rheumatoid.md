# Rheumatoid Arthritis  

## Definition / Overview  
- Systemic autoimmune disease characterized by inflammatory arthritis and extra-articular involvement.  
- Chronic inflammatory disorder caused by gene-environment interactions (e.g., tobacco use), primarily affecting synovial joints.  
- Typically begins in small peripheral joints symmetrically, progressing proximally if untreated.  
- Joint inflammation leads to cartilage loss and bone erosions.  
- Early RA: symptom duration <6 months; established RA: >6 months.  
- Untreated RA is progressive, associated with morbidity and increased mortality.  

## Epidemiology  
- Global prevalence: ~0.24% (Global Burden of Disease 2010 Study).  
- Higher prevalence in Western/Northern Europe, North America, Australia; lower in Central/South America, East Asia, Africa.  
- Annual incidence in the US and northern Europe: ~40 per 100,000 persons.  
- Female lifetime risk: 3.6% vs. 1.7% in males.  
- Peak incidence: 65–80 years.  
- Urban prevalence: 0.69% vs. rural 0.54%.  
- High-income countries: 0.49% prevalence vs. low-income: 0.35%.  
- Heritability: ~40–65% for seropositive RA, 20% for seronegative RA.  
- First-degree relative with RA: 3x increased risk; second-degree: 2x.  

## Etiology / Causes  
- **Genetic factors**:  
  - HLA-DRB1 alleles (*04, *01, *10) with "shared epitope."  
  - Polymorphisms in *PAD14, PTPN22, CTLA4, IL-2RA, STAT4, TRAF1, CCR6, IRF5*.  
  - SNPs in *PSORS1C1, PTPN22, MIR146A* linked to severe disease.  
- **Environmental factors**:  
  - Smoking (strongest risk factor, interacts with HLA-DRB1 in ACPA+ individuals).  
  - Silica, asbestos, textile dust, *Porphyromonas gingivalis* infection.  
  - Gut dysbiosis (increased *Actinobacteria, Collinsella, Eggerthella, Faecalibacterium*).  
- **Epigenetics**:  
  - Hypermethylation of *PTPN11* intron enhancer in RA fibroblast-like synoviocytes (FLS).  

## Pathophysiology  
- Autoimmunity begins at distant sites (lung, GI/gums) with modified proteins (e.g., citrullination via peptidyl arginine deiminase enzymes).  
- ACPA and RF autoantibodies precede clinical symptoms by years.  
- Synovium infiltrated by immune cells (T helper 1/17, B cells, macrophages, FLS).  
- FLS become invasive, producing matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) and RANKL, leading to osteoclast-mediated bone erosions.  
- Key cytokines: TNF, IL-6, GM-CSF, IL-17, IL-1, promoting inflammation and tissue destruction.  
- Complement activation by immune complexes exacerbates inflammation.  

## Clinical Presentation  
- **Musculoskeletal**:  
  - Insidious onset of joint pain/swelling, typically in small joints (hands/feet).  
  - Symmetric involvement, morning stiffness, progressive proximal spread.  
  - Advanced disease: ulnar deviation, swan neck/boutonniere deformities, reduced grip strength.  
- **Extra-articular**:  
  - Rheumatoid nodules (olecranon, pressure points).  
  - Interstitial lung disease (5–16% prevalence, associated with ACPA, anti-CarP, *MUC5B* variant).  
  - Secondary Sjögren’s syndrome (dry eyes/mouth).  
  - Rare: Felty syndrome (splenomegaly, neutropenia), vasculitis, scleritis.  

## Evaluation / Diagnosis  
- **Diagnostic criteria (2010 ACR/EULAR)**:  
  - Requires ≥6 points across 4 domains:  
    1. Joint involvement (large/small joints, counted differently).  
    2. Serology (RF, ACPA: low/high positive = 2/3 points).  
    3. Acute phase reactants (elevated CRP/ESR = 1 point).  
    4. Symptom duration ≥6 weeks = 1 point.  
- **Laboratory**:  
  - RF (sensitivity 69%, specificity 85%), ACPA (sensitivity 67%, specificity 95%).  
  - Anemia of chronic disease, thrombocytosis, neutropenia (Felty syndrome).  
  - Synovial fluid: leukocyte count 1,500–25,000/μL (predominantly PMNs), low C3/C4.  
- **Imaging**:  
  - X-rays: periarticular osteopenia, erosions in advanced disease.  
  - MRI/ultrasound: detect synovitis and erosions before radiographic changes.  

## Differential Diagnosis  
- Osteoarthritis  
- Psoriatic arthritis  
- Systemic lupus erythematosus  
- Sjögren syndrome  
- Polymyalgia rheumatic  
- Chronic gouty arthritis  
- Calcium pyrophosphate deposition disease  

## Management / Treatment  
- **Goals**: Early diagnosis, remission, prevent joint damage.  
- **Pharmacological**:  
  - **DMARDs**: Methotrexate (first-line), hydroxychloroquine, sulfasalazine, leflunomide.  
  - **Biologics**: TNF inhibitors (etanercept, infliximab), IL-6 inhibitors (tocilizumab), abatacept (T-cell costimulator), rituximab (anti-CD20).  
  - **JAK inhibitors**: Tofacitinib, baricitinib.  
- **Non-pharmacological**:  
  - Physical therapy, counseling, patient education.  
- **NSAIDs**: Symptom relief (e.g., celecoxib for GI safety).  
- **Corticosteroids**: Short-term bridge therapy or for flares.  
- **Monitoring**: Disease activity scores (DAS28, CDAI), screen for infections, hepatitis, TB.  
- **Vaccinations**: Pneumococcus, hepatitis, influenza, HPV, HZV, COVID-19.  

## Complications  
- Progressive joint damage/deformities.  
- Interstitial lung disease (ILD).  
- Accelerated atherosclerosis, coronary artery disease.  
- Lymphoma (non-Hodgkin’s, higher in active RA).  
- Osteoporosis, increased fracture risk.  
- Venous thromboembolism.  
- Depression, increased mortality (3x general population).  

## Prognosis  
- No cure; progressive without treatment.  
- Early treatment (≤6 months) improves function but mortality similar to late treatment.  
- 40% develop disability within 10 years.  
- Poor prognostic factors: high autoantibody titers, HLA-DRB1, early onset, female gender.  

## Prevention / Patient Education  
- Avoid smoking, maintain balanced diet, regular exercise, healthy weight.  
- Recognize early symptoms for timely intervention.  
- Regular monitoring for comorbidities (cardiovascular, lung, bone).  

## Interprofessional Considerations  
- Team includes rheumatologists, nurses, pharmacists, occupational therapists, social workers, mental health professionals.  
- Education on disease management, medication adherence, lifestyle modifications.  
- Coordination for comorbidities, rehabilitation, and psychosocial support.  
- Encourage support groups and mental health counseling for depression.
