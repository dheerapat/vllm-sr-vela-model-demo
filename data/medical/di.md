# Arginine Vasopressin Disorder (Diabetes Insipidus)

## Definition / Overview
- Arginine vasopressin disorder results from decreased release of antidiuretic hormone (ADH/AVP) or reduced renal response to AVP, causing electrolyte imbalances.  
- Two types: **Arginine Vasopressin Deficiency (AVP-D, formerly Central DI)** and **Arginine Vasopressin Resistance (AVP-R, formerly Nephrogenic DI)**.

## Epidemiology
- Affects approximately 1 in 25,000 people (0.004% global prevalence).  
- No gender preference.  
- Can develop at any age, with hereditary forms presenting earlier.

## Etiology / Causes  
### Arginine Vasopressin Deficiency (AVP-D)  
- **Idiopathic**: 30–50% of cases, often autoimmune (lymphocytic inflammation of pituitary stalk/posterior pituitary).  
- **Familial/Congenital**: Wolfram syndrome, PCSK1 gene deficiency, congenital hypopituitarism, septo-optic dysplasia.  
- **Acquired**: Neurosurgery (10–80% incidence post-craniopharyngioma/surgery), trauma, tumors (e.g., craniopharyngioma, metastases), infiltrative disorders (sarcoidosis, histiocytosis, autoimmune lymphocytic hypophysitis), hypoxic encephalopathy.  
- **Adipsic DI**: Lesions affecting hypothalamic osmoreceptors/thirst centers (e.g., craniopharyngioma, trauma).  

### Arginine Vasopressin Resistance (AVP-R)  
- **Hereditary**: X-linked (AVPR2 gene mutations, 90% of cases) or autosomal (aquaporin-2 gene mutations).  
- **Acquired**: Lithium (20% of chronic users), hypercalcemia (>11 mg/dL), hypokalemia, medications (cidofovir, amphotericin B, NSAIDs), renal diseases (sickle cell, polycystic kidney, Sjögren syndrome).  

### Gestational AVP-D  
- Caused by placental vasopressinase activity, degrading AVP.  

### Primary Polydipsia  
- Psychogenic, drug-induced (anticholinergics, nicotine), hypothalamic lesions, dipsogenic DI.

## Pathophysiology  
- **AVP-D**: Defective AVP synthesis/release from posterior pituitary due to hypothalamic/pituitary lesions. Serum AVP insufficient to concentrate urine.  
- **AVP-R**: Impaired renal response to AVP due to defective V2 receptors or aquaporin-2 channels, reducing water reabsorption in collecting ducts.  
- **Negative feedback loops**: Osmoregulation (osmolality >295 mOsm/kg triggers AVP release) and baroregulation (volume/pressure changes modulate AVP).

## Clinical Presentation  
- **General**: Polyuria (>50 mL/kg/day), dilute urine (osmolality <300 mOsm/L), polydipsia (up to 20 L/day), nocturia.  
- **Children**: Dehydration, constipation, vomiting, fever, failure to thrive.  
- **CNS involvement**: Headaches, visual defects.  
- **AVP-D complications**: Reduced bone mineral density, weakness, fatigue, myalgias.

## Evaluation / Diagnosis  
1. **Confirm hypotonic polyuria**:  
   - 24-hour urine volume (>2.5 L in adults).  
   - Urine osmolality <300 mOsm/L with high-normal serum osmolality.  
2. **Water deprivation test**:  
   - Discontinue if urine osmolality stabilizes or plasma osmolality >300 mOsm/kg.  
   - Administer desmopressin (DDAVP) to differentiate AVP-D (urine osmolality ↑ >100%) vs. AVP-R (minimal ↑).  
3. **Imaging**: MRI sella/suprasellar region for AVP-D.  
4. **Biomarkers**: Plasma copeptin (stable surrogate for AVP), hypertonic saline infusion test.  
5. **Rule out mimics**: Primary polydipsia, osmotic diuresis, glucosuria.

## Differential Diagnosis  
- Primary polydipsia  
- Glucosuria (uncontrolled diabetes mellitus)  
- Osmotic diuresis (mannitol, IV fluids, high-protein diet)  
- Ureteral obstruction, irritable bladder syndrome  
- Nocturia causes: Prostate hypertrophy, nocturnal fluid intake

## Management / Treatment  
### AVP-D  
- **DDAVP**: First-line (intranasal/oral/subcutaneous). Monitor for hyponatremia.  
- Adjuncts: Low-solute diet, thiazide diuretics, chlorpropamide, carbamazepine, NSAIDs.  

### AVP-R  
- Treat underlying cause (e.g., discontinue lithium, correct hypercalcemia).  
- Low-solute diet, thiazides, NSAIDs, DDAVP (limited efficacy).  

### Gestational AVP-D  
- DDAVP use; resolves postpartum.  

## Complications  
- Chronic dehydration, tachycardia, hypotension, weight loss, fatigue, headaches.  
- Kidney damage, brain injury (severe hyponatremia/hypernatremia).

## Prognosis  
- **AVP-D**: Depends on etiology (e.g., poor in Wolfram syndrome).  
- **AVP-R**: Excellent if reversible cause (e.g., lithium cessation).  
- **Gestational AVP-D**: Good prognosis with hydration.  
- Mortality risk in adipsic DI or untreated severe cases.

## Prevention / Patient Education  
- Medication adherence (e.g., DDAVP, thiazides).  
- Low-salt/protein diet.  
- Monitor hydration status; carry water.  
- Avoid triggers (e.g., excessive NSAIDs, heat exposure).  
- Education on recognizing dehydration (dizziness, confusion).

## Interprofessional Considerations  
- **Team**: Endocrinologists, neurosurgeons (for pituitary tumors), nurses (hydration monitoring), pharmacists (medication review, lithium risks), primary care.  
- Coordinated care for hydration management, electrolyte monitoring, and comorbidities.
