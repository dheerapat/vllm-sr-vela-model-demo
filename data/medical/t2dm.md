# Type 2 Diabetes  

## Definition / Overview  
- Chronic metabolic disorder characterized by persistent hyperglycemia due to impaired insulin secretion, resistance to insulin, or both.  
- Chronic hyperglycemia, combined with metabolic aberrations, causes organ damage, leading to microvascular (retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy) and macrovascular complications, increasing cardiovascular disease risk 2- to 4-fold.  

## Epidemiology  
- Global prevalence of diabetes mellitus (DM) was 425 million in 2017.  
- In 2015, ~10% of the US population had DM (7 million undiagnosed).  
- Prevalence increases with age: ~25% of adults >65 years have DM.  
- Rising due to obesity, physical inactivity, and energy-dense diets.  

## Etiology / Causes  
- **Type 1 Diabetes Mellitus (T1DM):** Autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells (5–10% of DM cases), often triggered by genetic susceptibility + environmental factors (viral infections, toxins).  
- **Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2DM):** Insulin resistance + beta-cell dysfunction (90% of DM cases), associated with obesity, physical inactivity, and age >45.  
- **Gestational Diabetes Mellitus (GDM):** Hyperglycemia first detected in pregnancy (7% of pregnancies; increases future T2DM risk).  
- **Monogenic Diabetes:** Single-gene mutations (e.g., MODY, neonatal diabetes; 1–5% of DM cases).  
- **Secondary Diabetes:** Caused by pancreatitis, hormonal disorders (e.g., Cushing’s), or drugs (e.g., corticosteroids).  

## Pathophysiology  
- **T1DM:** Autoimmune-mediated beta-cell destruction; associated with HLA genes (DR3-DQ2, DR4-DQ8). LADA (latent autoimmune diabetes in adults) is a slower-onset variant.  
- **T2DM:** Insulin resistance + progressive beta-cell dysfunction. Adipose tissue promotes insulin resistance via inflammatory mechanisms (e.g., FFA release, adipokine dysregulation). Involves incretin defects (e.g., GLP-1), hyperglucagonemia, and gut microbiota abnormalities.  

## Clinical Presentation  
- **Common Symptoms:** Polyuria, polydipsia, fatigue, recurrent infections, delayed wound healing, neuropathy (numbness/tingling), blurred vision.  
- **T1DM:** May present with diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) as initial manifestation (30% of cases).  

## Evaluation / Diagnosis  
- **Screening:**  
  - Annual screening for ages >40; more frequent with risk factors (obesity, family history, hypertension, etc.).  
- **Diagnostic Criteria:**  
  - Fasting Plasma Glucose (FPG) ≥126 mg/dL.  
  - 2-hour plasma glucose ≥200 mg/dL during 75-g oral glucose tolerance test (OGTT).  
  - HbA1c ≥6.5% (standardized to NGSP/DCCT assay; limited by conditions like anemia).  
  - **GDM:** 1-step (75-g OGTT with thresholds: fasting ≥92 mg/dL, 1-hour ≥180 mg/dL, 2-hour ≥153 mg/dL) or 2-step approach (50-g glucose challenge test followed by 100-g OGTT if positive).  

## Differential Diagnosis  
- Drug-induced hyperglycemia (e.g., corticosteroids, neuroleptics).  
- Genetic beta-cell/insulin action defects.  
- Metabolic syndrome.  
- Infections.  
- Endocrinopathies (e.g., acromegaly, Cushing’s, pheochromocytoma).  
- Iron overload (hemochromatosis).  
- Exocrine pancreatic disorders (e.g., pancreatitis, cystic fibrosis).  

## Management / Treatment  
- **Lifestyle:** Diet (low saturated fat, refined carbs; high fiber, monounsaturated fats) + aerobic exercise (90–150 min/week). Weight loss for obese patients.  
- **T2DM Pharmacotherapy:**  
  - First-line: Metformin.  
  - Second-line: Sulfonylureas, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 receptor agonists (e.g., liraglutide), SGLT2 inhibitors (e.g., empagliflozin), pioglitazone (if fatty liver), alpha-glucosidase inhibitors.  
  - Prefer agents without hypoglycemia risk (e.g., DPP-4i, SGLT2i, GLP-1 RA).  
- **T1DM:** Basal-bolus insulin or insulin pump therapy.  
- **Goals:**  
  - HbA1c <7%.  
  - BP <130/85 mmHg (ACE/ARB preferred).  
  - LDL-C <100 mg/dL (<70 mg/dL if atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease).  
  - Statins first-line; add ezetimibe/PCSK9 inhibitors if needed.  
- Regular monitoring: Fundoscopy, urine albumin, lipid panel.  

## Complications  
- **Acute:** Hypoglycemia, DKA, hyperosmolar hyperglycemic state, coma.  
- **Chronic Microvascular:** Retinopathy (4.4% vision-threatening in US), nephropathy (1% end-stage renal disease), neuropathy.  
- **Chronic Macrovascular:** Coronary artery disease, peripheral artery disease, cerebrovascular disease. Annual cardiovascular event rate: 1.4–4.7% in middle-aged individuals.  

## Prognosis  
- Increased atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (ASCVD) risk. Excess mortality ~15% higher in T2DM.  
- Improved outcomes with glycemic control, BP management, statins, and ACE/ARB therapy.  

## Prevention / Patient Education  
- Self-monitoring of blood glucose and symptoms of hypoglycemia (tachycardia, sweating, confusion; treat with 15–20g carbs).  
- Regular eye/foot exams, foot care.  
- Smoking cessation, limit alcohol.  
- Lifestyle modifications (diet, exercise, weight management).  

## Interprofessional Considerations  
- Team includes primary care, endocrinologist, ophthalmologist, nephrologist, cardiologist, vascular surgeon, and diabetes nurse.  
- Referrals for complications; education on lifestyle changes, medication adherence, and preventive care.
