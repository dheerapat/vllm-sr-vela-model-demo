# Sexually Transmitted Infections (STIs)

## Definition / Overview
- STIs involve transmission of organisms between sexual partners via oral, anal, or vaginal contact.  
- Include curable (gonorrhea, chlamydia, syphilis, trichomoniasis) and treatable but incurable conditions (herpes, HPV, HIV).  
- Symptoms broadly categorized as discharge/dysuria or ulcerative lesions.  

## Epidemiology
- **HPV**: Most common STI globally; ~80% of sexually active individuals infected at some point.  
- **US Data (2020)**:  
  - Chlamydia: 1.6 million cases.  
  - Gonorrhea: 677,769 cases (45% increase from 2016).  
  - Primary/Secondary Syphilis: 133,945 cases (52% increase).  
  - Congenital Syphilis: 2,148 cases (235% increase).  
- **Global Data**:  
  - 376 million new curable STIs annually (trichomoniasis, chlamydia, gonorrhea, syphilis).  
  - Over 500 million people globally infected with HSV-2.  

## Etiology / Causes
- **Chancroid**: *Haemophilus ducreyi*.  
- **Chlamydia**: *Chlamydia trachomatis* (serotypes D-K).  
- **Genital Herpes**: Herpes simplex virus 1 (HSV-1) or 2 (HSV-2).  
- **Gonorrhea**: *Neisseria gonorrhoeae*.  
- **Granuloma Inguinale**: *Klebsiella granulomatis*.  
- **HIV/AIDS**: Enveloped retrovirus.  
- **HPV**: Double-stranded DNA virus.  
- **Lymphogranuloma Venereum**: *Chlamydia trachomatis* (serovars L1, L2, L3).  
- **Mycoplasma genitalium**: *Mycoplasma genitalium*.  
- **Syphilis**: *Treponema pallidum*.  
- **Trichomoniasis**: *Trichomonas vaginalis*.  
- **Risk Factors**: Unprotected sex, multiple partners, history of STIs, sexual assault, substance use.  

## Pathophysiology
- STIs invade via mucosal abrasions; transmitted through sexual contact, IV drug use, or childbirth.  
- Bacterial, viral, or parasitic organisms trigger immune responses, causing tissue damage and systemic symptoms.  
- Untreated infections lead to complications (e.g., infertility, cancers, disseminated disease).  

## Clinical Presentation
- **Chancroid**: Painful genital ulcers, tender lymphadenopathy.  
- **Chlamydia**:  
  - Women: Discharge, bleeding, pelvic pain (often asymptomatic).  
  - Men: Dysuria, testicular pain.  
- **Genital Herpes**: Vesicular lesions, prodromal tingling/itching.  
- **Gonorrhea**: Similar to chlamydia; may include systemic symptoms (e.g., joint pain).  
- **HPV**: Asymptomatic or anogenital warts (condylomata acuminata).  
- **Syphilis**: Primary (painless chancre), secondary (rash, condylomata lata), latent, tertiary (neuro/cardiovascular complications).  
- **Trichomoniasis**: Foul-smelling discharge, "strawberry cervix."  

## Evaluation / Diagnosis
- **Chancroid**: Clinical diagnosis; Gram stain shows "school of fish" pattern.  
- **Chlamydia/Gonorrhea**: NAAT (vaginal swab, urine, urethral sample).  
- **Herpes**: NAAT, viral culture.  
- **Syphilis**: Nontreponemal (VDRL, RPR) + treponemal assays.  
- **HIV**: Antibody tests, PCR.  
- **PID**: Clinical criteria (cervical motion tenderness, discharge, elevated CRP/ESR).  
- **Pregnancy Screening (CDC)**:  
  - First visit: HIV, hepatitis B/C, syphilis.  
  - High-risk: Chlamydia, gonorrhea.  
  - Third trimester: Rescreen high-risk or untested women.  

## Differential Diagnosis
- Broad approach by organ system:  
  - **Cardiovascular/CNS**: HIV, syphilis, herpes.  
  - **GI/Respiratory**: HIV, HSV, chlamydia, gonorrhea, HPV.  
  - **Genitourinary/Integumentary**: All STIs.  

## Management / Treatment
- **Chancroid**: Azithromycin 1g or ceftriaxone 250mg IM.  
- **Chlamydia**: Doxycycline 100mg twice daily for 7 days or azithromycin 1g.  
- **Gonorrhea**: Ceftriaxone 500mg IM + doxycycline.  
- **Syphilis**: Benzathine penicillin G (dosage varies by stage).  
- **Trichomoniasis**: Metronidazole 500mg twice daily for 7 days (women); 2g single dose (men).  
- **Herpes**: Acyclovir, valacyclovir, or famciclovir for primary/suppressive therapy.  
- **HIV**: Antiretroviral therapy (HAART).  
- **PID**: Ceftriaxone + doxycycline + metronidazole.  

## Complications
- **Untreated STIs**: Infertility, systemic infections, scarring, cancers (HPV), congenital anomalies (syphilis, HIV).  
- **Pregnancy Risks**: Preterm labor, stillbirth, neonatal infections.  

## Prognosis
- Depends on early detection, treatment adherence, and access to healthcare.  
- Untreated cases may progress to severe morbidity/mortality.  

## Prevention / Patient Education
- **Vaccines**: HPV (2-dose series for ages 11-12, up to age 26).  
- **PrEP**: Emtricitabine/tenofovir for high-risk HIV individuals.  
- **Doxycycline PEP**: Post-exposure prophylaxis for chlamydia/gonorrhea/syphilis.  
- **Counseling**: Safe sex practices, regular screening, and behavior modification.  

## Interprofessional Considerations
- Collaboration among physicians, nurses, pharmacists, counselors, and public health specialists.  
- Coordination for patient education, treatment adherence, and resource allocation.  
- Centralized data collection to monitor STI trends and guide interventions.
