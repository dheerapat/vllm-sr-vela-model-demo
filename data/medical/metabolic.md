# Metabolic Syndrome

## Definition / Overview
- Metabolic syndrome is a cluster of metabolic abnormalities (central obesity, insulin resistance, hypertension, dyslipidemia) that increase the risk of atherosclerotic cardiovascular diseases and type II diabetes mellitus. Diagnosis requires ≥3 of these abnormalities.

## Epidemiology
- Prevalence: ~20% of Americans and Europeans affected.  
- Increased globally, paralleling rising obesity rates.  
- Higher prevalence in South Asian Americans despite lower obesity rates compared to non-Hispanic whites.  
- Affects 3% of children and 5% of adolescents globally, with higher rates in low-income countries.  
- Prevalence rises with age, peaking at ~40% in the 6th decade of life. Slightly more prevalent in women in some ethnic groups.  

## Etiology / Causes
- Multifactorial: genetic predisposition and environmental/lifestyle factors.  
- Key contributors: obesity, sedentary lifestyle, unhealthy diet, excessive calorie intake.  
- Fetal and early postnatal environmental factors (e.g., maternal lifestyle, intrauterine/postnatal nutrition) influence risk.  

## Pathophysiology
- Central mechanism: visceral obesity → insulin resistance and chronic inflammation.  
- Insulin resistance impairs glucose metabolism, promotes fat deposition, and drives hypertension and dyslipidemia.  
- Adipose tissue releases pro-inflammatory cytokines (e.g., TNF-α, leptin, resistin) and free fatty acids, exacerbating insulin resistance and endothelial dysfunction.  
- Contributes to atherosclerosis, vascular stiffness, and thrombogenicity via elevated fibrinogen and PAI-1.  

## Clinical Presentation
- **Signs**: Abdominal obesity (elevated BMI/waist circumference), acanthosis nigricans, xanthomas.  
- **Symptoms**: Polyuria, polydipsia, polyphagia (suggestive of diabetes). Hypertension may be asymptomatic.  

## Evaluation / Diagnosis
- **Diagnostic Criteria**:  
  - Waist circumference: >40 inches (men), >35 inches (women).  
  - Fasting triglycerides ≥150 mg/dL.  
  - HDL: <40 mg/dL (men), <50 mg/dL (women).  
  - Fasting glucose ≥100 mg/dL.  
  - Blood pressure: SBP ≥130 mmHg or DBP ≥85 mmHg.  
- **Laboratory Tests**: Lipid panel, fasting glucose/HbA1c, basic metabolic panel, C-reactive protein, LFTs, thyroid studies, uric acid.  
- **Imaging**: Coronary calcium score for ASCVD risk assessment; stress testing for symptomatic patients.  

## Differential Diagnosis
- Hypothyroidism, polycystic ovarian syndrome (PCOS), pheochromocytoma, Cushing syndrome, acromegaly.  
- Secondary causes of hypertension/dyslipidemia (e.g., renal disease, coarctation of the aorta).  

## Management / Treatment
- **Lifestyle Modifications**:  
  - 7–10% weight loss over 12 months; target BMI <25 kg/m².  
  - ≥150 minutes/week moderate-intensity or ≥70 minutes/week high-intensity exercise.  
  - Diet: Mediterranean-style (vegetables, fruits, whole grains, fish); avoid processed foods, saturated fats, sodium.  
  - Smoking cessation, limited alcohol, sleep hygiene.  
- **Pharmacologic Therapy**:  
  - Dyslipidemia: Statins or per guidelines.  
  - Hypertension: Antihypertensives tailored to comorbidities.  
  - Insulin resistance/diabetes: Metformin, DPP-4 inhibitors, GLP-1 agonists, pioglitazone.  
- **Surgical Management**: Bariatric surgery (e.g., gastric bypass) for BMI ≥40 kg/m² or ≥35 kg/m² with comorbidities.  

## Complications
- Cardiovascular events (MI, stroke, heart failure) with 2-fold increased risk.  
- Type II diabetes mellitus (near-universal progression) with microvascular complications.  
- Obesity-related: obstructive sleep apnea, pulmonary hypertension, osteoarthritis.  
- Increased risk of malignancies (kidney, gallbladder, colon, prostate).  

## Prognosis
- Poor prognosis driven by cardiovascular complications.  
- Recent advances in ASCVD management have improved outcomes.  

## Prevention / Patient Education
- Target BMI <25 kg/m².  
- Regular physical activity, Mediterranean diet, smoking cessation, alcohol moderation.  
- Sleep hygiene and treatment of sleep apnea.  

## Interprofessional Considerations
- Team includes cardiologist, endocrinologist, dietitian, physical therapist, pharmacist, neurologist, bariatric surgeon, nurse practitioner, social worker.  
- Emphasis on patient education, adherence monitoring, and coordinated care.
